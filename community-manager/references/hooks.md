# Hook library

The first line decides whether anything else gets read. Write three, pick one.

These are **formulas, not scripts**. Fill them from the brand profile: the audience's real
frustration, in the audience's own words. A formula filled with generic language produces
generic content, which is the failure mode this file exists to prevent.

Every bracketed value is a placeholder you must fill from something real, or list in the
output as needing the user's input. Never invent a number to complete a formula.

## Curiosity gap

Promise a specific insight, withhold the payoff. The specificity is what makes it work; a
vague promise is ignored.

- "We looked at [N] [things]. The ones that [succeeded] had [one thing] in common."
- "The real reason [common outcome] happens. It is not [the obvious cause]."
- "There is a pattern in every [X] that works. Almost nobody talks about it."

**Rule**: the payoff has to be in the post. A hook that oversells what follows costs more
trust than the impressions it earns.

## Contrarian

Take a real position against something your audience believes. It has to be a position you
would defend in the replies, because you will have to.

- "[Common advice] is wrong. Here is what actually happens."
- "You do not need [thing everyone says you need]. You need [the real thing]."
- "Stop [common behaviour]. [Consequence they have not considered]."

**Rule**: contrarian, not contrarian-for-engagement. If you do not believe it, it reads as
a stunt, and the audience you want is the audience that notices.

## Question

Genuinely answerable, in one line, from personal experience. "Thoughts?" is not a question.

- "What is the [thing] you have been sitting on for months?"
- "Honest question: how many [X] do you have right now?"
- "What is actually stopping you from [action]?"

## Social proof

**Only with real numbers you have been given.** If you do not have one, use a different
formula rather than a placeholder that will get published as-is.

- "[Real number] [users] did [action] this week."
- "[Customer, with permission] went from [real before] to [real after]."

**Rule**: attribution and permission for any named person. No composites presented as real
people. No rounding up.

## Story

The highest performing format on most platforms, and the hardest to fake.

- "[Time ago] I [did the thing]. [What I did not expect]."
- "[Person] [did something small]. [Outcome that followed]."
- "I almost [gave up on it] [N] times. Then [the turn]."

**Rule**: it has to have happened. A useful true story about a small thing beats an
impressive invented one, and the invented one is a liability the first time someone asks
for details.

## Direct value

No cleverness. Say what they get.

- "The [checklist] that [specific outcome]."
- "How to [outcome] in [timeframe] without [the expensive thing]."
- "[N] signals that [X is ready], and [N] that it is not."

**Rule**: deliver it in the post. If the value is behind a link, this is an ad, and it
should be written and disclosed as one.

## Pattern interrupt

Short, blunt, breaks the scroll rhythm.

- "Stop [building] [thing nobody asked for]."
- "Your [X] does not need [Y]. It needs [Z]."
- "You do not need permission to [action]."

**Rule**: use sparingly. A feed of these reads as shouting, and it ages badly.

## Never

- Fake urgency: countdowns that do not count down, spot counts you made up.
- Manufactured outrage.
- "You will not believe" and every variant.
- Engagement bait with nothing behind it.
- Anyone else's post with the nouns swapped.
