# Platform playbooks

**These are heuristics with a shelf life.** Platform distribution changes without notice,
and anything here can be stale. Treat it as a starting position: what stays true is format
and audience expectation, what drifts is the ranking behaviour. When a recommendation
carries real stakes, verify current behaviour and say what you checked.

The parts that have not changed in years: the hook decides everything, stories beat feature
lists, and replying to people beats posting at them.

## X / Twitter

Builders, developers and founders congregate here, so it is usually primary for developer
tools and usually not for consumer or design products.

**Format**: short and punchy. Threads when there is genuine depth, never to pad. Quote
posts to add a take rather than to repeat.

**What tends to work**: replies and conversation over broadcast. Building in public with
real numbers. A position stated plainly. Questions that are actually answerable in one
line, not "thoughts?".

**Craft**
- One idea per post. If it needs a second idea, it needs a second post.
- The first line has to work with no context, because that is how it will be seen.
- Thread rule: post one has to earn post two. Number them only if order matters.
- Engage before you post. Thirty minutes of real replies does more than the post.

**Length**: under 280 even when more is available. Shorter posts get read.

## LinkedIn

Professional audience, and the most punishing platform for corporate voice.

**Format**: personal narrative. First two lines above the fold are the entire game. Short
paragraphs, one or two sentences, with white space doing the work.

**Craft**
- **Never open with "we are excited to announce"** or an equivalent. Open with tension,
  a specific moment, or a claim someone might disagree with.
- One idea per post. Depth beats breadth here more than anywhere.
- **No feature changelogs.** They read as ads and get buried. Reframe each feature as
  something that happened to a person.
- Link in the first comment or at the end. Strong content survives a link; weak content
  does not get rescued by hiding one.
- End with a genuine question. A forced one is worse than none.
- The first hour matters. Post when your audience is actually awake and be there to reply.

**A note on measuring this**: the gap between a story-shaped post and a feature-list post
on the same product in the same week is routinely close to an order of magnitude in reach.
Run that comparison on your own account rather than trusting anyone's numbers, including
these: two posts, same week, same product, one narrative and one list. The result will
calibrate your instinct better than any guide.

**Length**: 800 to 1,500 characters is the usual sweet spot. Longer works when it is a
story people finish.

## Instagram

Visual first. Saves and shares matter more than likes, and DM shares matter most.

**Formats**
- **Carousels**: five to eight slides, educational, worth saving. Slide one is a bold hook
  and nothing else. Last slide is the CTA.
- **Reels**: 15 to 30 seconds, text on screen since most watch muted, one idea.
- **Stories**: polls and questions. This is where you talk to people, not at them.

**Hashtags**: a mix of branded, niche and broad. Fifteen to twenty. Niche outperforms
broad, because broad tags are a firehose nobody browses.

## TikTok and short video

Raw beats polished, consistency beats virality. The first two seconds decide. Text on
screen always. Reply to comments with video, which is the cheapest distribution on the
platform.

## Newsletter

The only channel you own. Everything else is rented and can be repriced overnight.

One idea per send. A subject line written as carefully as a hook. Consistent cadence beats
frequent cadence. Make replies easy and read them, since a newsletter reply is the highest
signal feedback in this whole list.

## Reddit, Hacker News and forums

**Participate for months before you post about your product**, and even then, only where
it genuinely answers the question. These communities detect and punish marketing faster
than any other channel, and the punishment is durable.

When you do mention what you built: disclose that it is yours, in the first line, without
being asked.

## Cross-posting

Do not paste the same text everywhere. The idea travels, the format does not. One idea
becomes a thread on X, a narrative post on LinkedIn, a carousel on Instagram and a section
in the newsletter, each written for that audience's expectation.
